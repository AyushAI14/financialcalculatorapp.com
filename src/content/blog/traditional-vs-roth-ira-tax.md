When setting up an Individual Retirement Account (IRA), you face one primary choice: **Traditional or Roth?**

The decision boils down to one simple question: **When do you want to pay taxes?**

---

## 1. Traditional IRA: Tax Break Now
With a Traditional IRA, your contributions are generally **tax-deductible**. 

* **How it works:** If you earn $70,000 this year and contribute $7,000 to a Traditional IRA, the government calculates your taxes as if you only earned $63,000. 
* **The Catch:** Your money grows tax-deferred, but when you withdraw it in retirement, the withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income.

**Best for:** People currently in their peak earning years who expect to be in a lower tax bracket when they retire.

---

## 2. Roth IRA: Tax Break Later
With a Roth IRA, you get **no tax deduction today**. You contribute post-tax dollars.

* **How it works:** You pay income tax on your earnings now, contribute to the Roth, and your investments grow.
* **The Reward:** When you withdraw your money in retirement (after age 59½), both your contributions and all of your investment growth are **100% tax-free**.

**Best for:** Younger workers, lower earners, or anyone who expects to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement.

---

## 3. Which saves you more money?
It comes down to a comparison of your **Current Tax Bracket** versus your **Retirement Tax Bracket**:

* **Current Tax Rate > Future Tax Rate:** Choose **Traditional**. You save more by avoiding high taxes today.
* **Current Tax Rate < Future Tax Rate:** Choose **Roth**. You pay lower taxes today to avoid higher taxes tomorrow.
* **Current Tax Rate = Future Tax Rate:** Mathematically, the two plans are identical! However, Roth IRAs have additional benefits, like no Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs).
