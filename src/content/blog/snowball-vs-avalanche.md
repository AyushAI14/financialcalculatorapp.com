If you are carrying multiple debts—such as credit cards, student loans, or auto loans—you have likely heard of the two main strategies for paying them off: **Debt Snowball** and **Debt Avalanche**.

Both methods require you to pay the minimum payment on all debts, and then focus any extra monthly cash toward a single "target" debt. But how they select that target is very different.

## The Debt Snowball Method
Popularized by personal finance figures like Dave Ramsey, the **Debt Snowball** method prioritizes **balance sizes**:

1. List your debts from smallest balance to largest balance.
2. Focus all extra budget on paying off the **smallest debt first**.
3. Once that is paid off, roll its entire minimum payment and surplus budget into the next smallest debt.

### Why it works
* **Psychological Momentum**: Getting quick wins by completely wiping out small accounts provides a strong sense of achievement.
* **Simplification**: You have fewer open accounts to track quickly.

## The Debt Avalanche Method
The **Debt Avalanche** method focuses strictly on **interest rates**:

1. List your debts from highest interest rate (APR) to lowest interest rate.
2. Focus all extra budget on paying off the **highest interest rate debt first**.
3. Once that is paid off, roll the payments into the debt with the next highest rate.

### Why it works
* **Mathematical Superiority**: By paying down high-rate debt first, you prevent compounding interest fees from accumulating, saving you the maximum amount of money.
* **Shorter Timeframe**: Paying less interest means more of your budget goes to principal, reducing the time needed to become debt-free.

## Which Wins?
Mathematically, the Avalanche method wins every single time because it minimizes interest paid. However, human psychology is not always driven by math. Many savers fail to stay motivated during the long periods it takes to pay off a large, high-rate balance first. If you need behavioral reinforcement, the Snowball is a great fit. If you want to optimize your savings, choose the Avalanche.
