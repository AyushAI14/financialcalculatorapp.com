Federal student loan repayment can feel overwhelming, but the government offers a safety net called **Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) plans**. 

Instead of demanding a fixed payment that you can't afford, these plans cap your monthly payments at a percentage of your **discretionary income**.

Here is how the three main plans—SAVE, PAYE, and IBR—work and how they differ.

---

## 1. What is Discretionary Income?
The government doesn't expect you to pay student loans using the money you need for rent, groceries, and healthcare. 

To determine what you can afford, they subtract a portion of the **Federal Poverty Guideline** (based on your family size) from your Adjusted Gross Income (AGI). The money left over is your **discretionary income**. Your monthly payment is calculated as a percentage of this amount.

---

## 2. Comparing the Plans: SAVE, PAYE, and IBR

### A. The SAVE Plan (Saving on a Valuable Education)
SAVE replaced the older REPAYE plan and is the most generous IDR plan:
* **Affordability:** Protects income up to **225%** of the poverty line (meaning you keep more of your money).
* **Payment Cap:** Payments are capped at **5%** for undergraduate loans and **10%** for graduate loans of your discretionary income.
* **Interest Benefit:** If your monthly payment doesn't cover the interest that accumulates, the government waives the rest. Your balance won't grow!

### B. The PAYE Plan (Pay As You Earn)
* **Affordability:** Protects income up to **150%** of the poverty line.
* **Payment Cap:** capped at **10%** of discretionary income.
* **Forgiveness:** Remaining balance is forgiven after **20 years** of payments.

### C. The IBR Plan (Income-Based Repayment)
* **Affordability:** Protects income up to **150%** of the poverty line.
* **Payment Cap:** capped at **10%** or **15%** depending on when you first borrowed.
* **Forgiveness:** Remaining balance is forgiven after **20 or 25 years**.

Monthly payment requirements vary dramatically based on your income, family size, and student loan balances.
