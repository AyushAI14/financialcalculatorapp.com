## Part 3: What Was Einstein *Actually* Calculating?

While the meme shows Albert Einstein tapping away on an iPhone calculator, digital calculators did not exist during Einstein's lifetime (he passed away in 1955, while the first electronic pocket calculators like the Sharp QT-8D and Texas Instruments models didn't arrive until the late 1960s and 1970s).

So how did the real Einstein calculate his groundbreaking theories?

### 1. The Slide Rule and Fountain Pen
Einstein relied primarily on **slide rules** (analog mechanical calculators using logarithmic scales), mechanical adding machines, and good old-fashioned fountain pens on reams of scrap paper. 

He famously conducted **"Gedankenexperimenten"** (mental thought experiments)—visualizing what it would look like to ride alongside a beam of light—before translating those concepts into complex differential geometry and tensor calculus.

### 2. His Most Famous Equation: $E = mc^2$
The mass-energy equivalence formula proves that energy ($E$) equals mass ($m$) multiplied by the speed of light squared ($c^2$). Because the speed of light is approximately $300{,}000\text{ km/s}$, squaring it ($9 \times 10^{16}$) reveals that a tiny amount of matter contains an incomprehensible reservoir of energy.

### 3. Einstein's Take on Compound Interest
Einstein is famously credited with observing the power of exponential growth in finance:

> *"Compound interest is the eighth wonder of the world. He who understands it, earns it; he who doesn't, pays it."*

While historians debate whether Einstein actually uttered those exact words, the mathematical principle is undeniable: exponential compounding is the mathematical engine behind both atomic reactions and generational wealth creation.

If you are opening up your calculator app—whether you feel like Einstein or just a "blud" trying to make sense of your finances—here are the calculations that actually change your life:

### 1. The Rule of 72 (Doubling Your Money)
Want to know how fast your savings or investments will double? Divide 72 by your annual interest rate:
$$\text{Years to Double} = \frac{72}{\text{Annual Return %}}$$
* At **7% annual stock market return**: $72 \div 7 \approx 10.3\text{ years}$ to double your money.
* At **10% return** (historical S&P 500 average): $72 \div 10 = 7.2\text{ years}$ to double.

### 2. Safe Retirement Payout (The 4% Rule)
Wondering how big a nest egg you need to retire comfortably without running out of money?
$$\text{Annual Retirement Budget} \times 25 = \text{Target Retirement Nest Egg}$$
* If you need **$60,000/year** in retirement: $60{,}000 \times 25 = \mathbf{\$1{,}500{,}000}$.
* That $1,500,000 portfolio generates **$5,000/month** in sustainable income using the 4% safe withdrawal rule.
